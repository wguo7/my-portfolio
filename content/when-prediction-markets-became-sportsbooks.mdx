## Abstract

On January 23, 2025, Kalshi, a federally regulated exchange previously oriented toward markets on elections and economic data, officially launched sports betting. Within two months, sports contracts accounted for more than half of all volume traded on the platform.

This article examines whether the arrival of a sports-betting user base was accompanied by measurable changes in trading behavior in the platform's non-sports markets. The question is well posed because the platform imposes no separation between products: an account opened to bet on sports can trade contracts on inflation or elections at no additional cost. The analysis draws on the platform's complete trade history, approximately 593 million trades from mid-2021 through August 2026, and reports three findings.

1. **Composition.** Among contracts priced at 1 to 20 cents, corresponding to outcomes the market judges less than 20 percent likely, the share of taker order flow buying the longshot side rose from 52.1 percent before the launch to 65.7 percent after. The increase appears in political, financial, and miscellaneous market categories alike.
2. **Returns.** The realized excess return on longshot taker positions deteriorated sharply. In the cleanest comparison, markets that resolve within 60 days of creation, such positions earned 8.3 cents per dollar before the launch and lost 21.6 cents per dollar after. The direction of the bias reversed.
3. **Aggregate losses.** Relative to pre-launch loss rates, traders lost approximately $13.0 million more than expected in the post-launch period. Because a small number of very large markets dominate aggregate dollar outcomes, this figure carries wide statistical uncertainty and is reported as accounting rather than as a hypothesis test.

Twelve headline tests were conducted, and every reported significance level is adjusted for the number of tests performed. Eight of the twelve survive the adjustment. Results that failed are reported alongside those that succeeded.

## 1. Introduction

The economic case for prediction markets rests on the composition of their participants. Prices aggregate information effectively when the typical trader holds a genuine view about the underlying event; they do so less effectively when the typical trader is purchasing a small chance of a large payoff, in the manner of a lottery participant.

The betting literature summarizes the lottery-like pattern with the **favorite-longshot bias**: the extensively documented tendency of bettors to pay too much for unlikely outcomes and too little for likely ones. The strength of this bias in a given market serves as a compact indicator of who is trading in it.

Kalshi's sports launch provides an unusually clean setting in which to observe a change in participant composition. Before 2025, the platform's users had self-selected into forecasting markets. The launch of January 23, 2025 introduced a product with mass-market appeal, and the shared account infrastructure allowed habits formed in one product to carry into the other. If sports-betting behavior spilled into the forecasting markets, the effect should be observable in three measurable quantities: the side of the market that aggressive order flow takes, the distribution of that flow across the price range, and its subsequent realized returns. This article measures all three.

The design is an observational, platform-level event study rather than a controlled experiment. Section 5 sets out the identification concerns, including pre-existing trends, and the checks that bound them.

**Terminology.** On every trade, the **maker** is the party whose order was resting on the book, and the **taker** is the counterparty who crossed the spread to trade against it. Takers skew toward retail participants; makers skew toward professional liquidity providers. A **longshot** denotes a contract priced at 1 to 20 cents, an implied probability below 20 percent. The **excess return** on a resolved position is the payout minus the entry price, divided by the entry price, with each dollar weighted equally.

## 2. Data

The analysis is based on what is, to the author's knowledge, the largest public dataset of prediction-market activity, assembled with an [open-source collection framework](https://github.com/Jon-Becker/prediction-market-analysis). Following the refresh conducted for this article, the dataset comprises approximately **593 million Kalshi trades and 30 million market records** spanning June 2021 through August 4, 2026. Each trade record identifies the taker side, the prices of both contracts, and the quantity; each market record supplies the resolution outcome and its timing.

Three properties of the collection process shaped the research design. The first is of independent interest to researchers working with this platform.

**Retention.** Kalshi's public data interface returns trades from approximately the most recent 72 days only; older history is deleted. This was verified directly: a market with millions of recorded trades returns data for May 26, 2026 and returns nothing for May 5, 2026. As a consequence, the assembled history is complete through May 1, 2026, contains an unrecoverable 23-day gap (the platform purged the records before they could be collected), and is complete again from May 25, 2026 onward. Kalshi's history cannot be reconstructed retrospectively; it must be recorded continuously, as this project has done since 2021.

**Comparability across regimes.** By mid-2026 the platform listed tens of millions of short-lived markets on cryptocurrency prices, with durations measured in minutes, and cleared on the order of eight million trades per day, roughly one hundred times its 2024 rate. Data from this later era differs from the historical sample in both platform character and collection method. Accordingly, **no statistical comparison in this article crosses the May 2026 boundary**. All tests use the consistently collected sample ending May 1, 2026, combined with market resolutions recorded through August 2026. The resolution update is itself material: thousands of markets that remained open in the spring have since settled, which strengthens the returns estimates. The post-gap era is described separately in Section 6.

**Sample rules.** All results are restricted to markets with at least 100 contracts of lifetime volume, the rule applied throughout the historical collection, so that the measured universe remains stable over time. Classification of markets as sports or non-sports uses a curated list of ticker prefixes matched longest-first. Keyword-based classification was evaluated and rejected during auditing; among other errors, it assigns UKRAINE-prefixed markets to a weather category because the name contains the string RAIN.

## 3. Research design

**Event date.** The event date is January 23, 2025, the official sports launch. A small number of sports markets existed from October 2024, but their volume was negligible. Following the official launch, growth was rapid: sports exceeded 25 percent of platform contract volume in the week of March 17, 2025, exceeded 50 percent the following week, and reached a maximum weekly share of 92 percent in late 2025.

![Kalshi sports weekly volume, cumulative share of post-launch sports volume, and sports share of total platform volume, 2024 to 2026](/img/blog/kalshi/sports-ramp.png)

**Treatment of the 2024 election.** The pre-launch period includes the 2024 United States election, the highest-volume and most retail-intensive episode in the platform's history before the launch. For the returns results this inclusion is conservative: election-period losses raise the pre-launch average loss rate, which reduces the measured post-launch deterioration. As a robustness check, all results were re-estimated with the pre-launch period restricted to the twelve months preceding the event date. Under that restriction the composition shift is larger (+16.8 percentage points rather than +13.6) and the returns deterioration remains substantial (−28.8 percentage points, p = 0.003). Both specifications appear in the published results.

**Statistical treatment.** Weekly market data exhibits persistence: adjacent weeks resemble one another, which can overstate the significance of naive comparisons. All time-series tests therefore use Newey-West standard errors, with bootstrap checks as a complement. The within-market comparison uses market fixed effects with standard errors clustered by market, and aggregate dollar figures are evaluated with a market-level bootstrap, because all dollars staked in a single market settle together and cannot be treated as independent observations. Because twelve headline tests were conducted, every reported significance level carries a Holm multiple-testing correction. Exact procedures appear in the methods note at the end of the article; the complete output of sixty tests, including failures, is available in the public repository.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Composition of taker order flow

Consider every non-sports trade in which the YES contract was priced between 1 and 20 cents, and ask whether the taker bought the longshot YES side or sold against it. Before the sports launch, takers bought the longshot side **52.1 percent** of the time. After the launch, the figure is **65.7 percent**. Adjusted for multiple testing, the probability of observing a shift of this size by chance is approximately one in one million.

![Longshot YES-taker share around the sports launch, and taker excess returns by price range before versus after](/img/blog/kalshi/composition-and-flb.png)

Three additional results establish the breadth of the change.

- **The shift appears in every market category.** Under the curated classification, the increase is +8.2 percentage points in political markets, +9.9 in financial and economic markets, and +5.9 in the residual category, each significant after adjustment.
- **The shift is not confined to the cheapest contracts.** Across the full 1-to-99-cent price range, taker buying tilted toward cheaper contracts after the launch, and the change in this relationship is itself highly significant.
- **The shift operates through new markets rather than existing ones.** When each market is compared only with itself, restricting attention to markets that traded on both sides of the event date and accounting for each market's age, the estimated change is −1.2 percentage points and statistically indistinguishable from zero. The platform-wide shift therefore derives from newly created markets and the flow arriving in them, not from deterioration within established markets. This is the pattern expected if the mechanism is the arrival of new participants and new market types rather than a repricing of existing instruments.

A further specification is used again below: restricting both periods to markets resolved within 60 days of creation, the composition shift is **+8.0 percentage points** and remains highly significant.

### 4.2 Taker returns on longshot positions

A notable feature of the pre-launch platform is the near-absence of the favorite-longshot bias. In the 60-day-resolution subsample, longshot taker positions earned a positive excess return of **+8.3 cents per dollar** before the launch. Nearly every betting market examined in the literature exhibits substantial negative returns to comparable positions; Kalshi's early users were, in this respect, unusually careful about the pricing of low-probability events.

After the launch, this pattern reversed on every measure examined.

| Specification | Before | After | Statistical assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly average taker excess return, entries at 1-20 cents | −2.5% | −42.2% | significant after adjustment (p ≈ 6×10⁻⁵) |
| Pooled dollar-weighted excess return | −37.8% | −47.6% | reported as description |
| Markets resolving within 60 days only | +8.3% | −21.6% | significant after adjustment (p ≈ 10⁻⁶) |
| Post-launch period beginning September 2025 | | | significant (p = 0.003; conservative check p = 0.028) |

The rows serve distinct purposes. The first is the formal time-series test, treating each week as one observation. The second weights every dollar equally and is the appropriate measure of aggregate trader experience; it differs from the first because a small number of high-volume election weeks dominate dollar totals. The third is the cleanest like-for-like comparison, and it shows the sign of the returns reversing. The fourth demonstrates that the result does not depend on the immediate post-election months: it holds when the post-launch period begins in September 2025, by which point sports constituted roughly 74 percent of platform volume.

An alternative explanation warrants examination: the contracts themselves may have become worse purchases after the launch, for reasons unrelated to the identity of the purchasers. Under that explanation, the passive side of the same trades would exhibit a mirror-image improvement for mechanical reasons. The data show instead that the maker-taker return gap widened sharply after the launch, which indicates a change in the composition of demand rather than a change in the underlying events.

### 4.3 Aggregate losses

Applying pre-launch loss rates, computed within each price range, to post-launch trading volumes yields expected losses of approximately **$12.7 million** in non-sports markets after January 2025. Realized losses were approximately **$25.7 million**. The difference, roughly **$13.0 million**, measures the additional cost associated with the post-launch shift in behavior.

The composition of the excess is informative. Approximately $5.4 million arises in the 1-20 cent range. The largest single component, $16.2 million, arises in the 61-80 cent range, where traders earned positive returns before the launch and approximately zero after. The pre-launch user base's advantage on likely outcomes disappeared together with its discipline on unlikely ones.

![Loss per dollar wagered by price range before versus after, and cumulative dollar losses on non-sports markets](/img/blog/kalshi/retail-losses.png)

The appropriate degree of confidence in these dollar figures requires care. An earlier version of this analysis attached very strong statistical significance to the excess, using a procedure that implicitly treated each dollar as an independent observation. That procedure is invalid: all dollars staked in a market settle together, so the correct unit of evidence is the market, and there are far fewer markets than dollars. Evaluated at the market level, the excess is not statistically distinguishable from zero (p ≈ 0.27), because aggregate outcomes are dominated by a small number of very large markets. The findings of Sections 4.1 and 4.2 constitute the statistically supported results; the dollar figure represents the magnitude those results plausibly imply, and it is presented as accounting rather than as evidence.

## 5. Identification concerns

**Pre-existing trends.** The longshot share of taker flow was already rising during 2024, before the event date, and the twelve-month pre-launch trend is statistically significant for both primary outcomes. Much of this drift coincides with the election cycle. Three checks bound the concern. A placebo exercise that imposes an artificial event date inside the stable 2023 period detects no effect (p = 0.66). The results are unchanged when the post-launch period begins in September 2025, well after the election cycle. The composition shift also survives the exclusion of the largest post-launch markets, so no single prominent market accounts for it. A reader who attributes part of the measured magnitude to the persistence of election-era participants, rather than to the arrival of sports bettors, holds a defensible partial interpretation; the twelve-month-pre and late-post-window estimates bound its possible size.

**Absence of a cross-platform control.** The natural alternative design would compare Kalshi's markets against Polymarket's as a control group. That design was constructed and then set aside on the basis of its own diagnostics: the two platforms' volumes were already diverging significantly before the event date, so the comparison's central assumption fails. Reporting an estimate from a design whose foundation is rejected would be less informative than reporting none.

**Unresolved markets at the sample boundary.** Return calculations require known outcomes, and markets unresolved at the end of the sample are excluded, which could in principle distort estimates near the boundary. The 60-day-resolution specification exists to address this concern, and the principal returns result survives it.

**Fees.** Trading fees are not modeled. Their inclusion would reduce every reported return, so the documented deterioration is understated in this respect.

## 6. The platform after May 2026

The post-gap data, covering May 25 through August 4, 2026, describes a platform transformed a second time. Kalshi now clears roughly eight million trades per day; competitive video gaming accounts for approximately 23 percent of contract volume, and short-duration cryptocurrency markets dominate the non-sports segment. In this later era, takers buy the longshot side **72.9 percent** of the time, a higher figure than any observed in the estimation sample, and such positions lose approximately 17 cents per dollar. Because both the platform and the collection method differ across the boundary, these figures are presented as description rather than as tests. They give no indication that the patterns documented above are reversing.

## 7. Conclusion

For its first four years, Kalshi's forecasting markets were traded by participants who priced unlikely events with unusual care; the most robust bias in the betting literature was largely absent from the platform. In the months following the January 2025 sports launch, taker order flow in those same market categories came to resemble sportsbook flow, tilted heavily toward longshots and paying substantially for the tilt.

The change arrived through new markets and new participants rather than through deterioration within existing markets, consistent with the most direct explanation: the platform's new customers brought their trading habits with them. These findings do not by themselves impair prediction-market prices as forecasts, since professional liquidity provision continues to discipline quotes. They do document a measurable worsening in the outcomes of ordinary participants at precisely the moment the industry found its mass-market product. Both facts belong in any assessment of the social value of regulated event markets.

The code, the data-collection infrastructure, and the complete set of sixty statistical results, including those that failed, are available in the [public repository](https://github.com/Jon-Becker/prediction-market-analysis).

*Methods note, for technical readers. Event date 2025-01-23. Weekly series: Newey-West (lag 4) regressions on a post indicator, with moving-block bootstrap p-values as a complement. Within-market specification: market fixed effects, market-age control, standard errors clustered by market, absorbed effects counted in the degrees-of-freedom correction. Dollar aggregates: market-clustered bootstrap, 1,000 draws. Multiple testing: Holm-Bonferroni across the twelve-test headline family, of which eight survive. Returns follow Buergi, Deng and Whelan (2025): (payout − entry price) / entry price, dollar-weighted, with the taker side taken from the aggressor flag on each trade. Sample: trades through 2026-05-01 under a stable 100-contract minimum-volume rule; resolutions as of 2026-08-04. Observational platform-level event study; causal interpretation is subject to Section 5.*
