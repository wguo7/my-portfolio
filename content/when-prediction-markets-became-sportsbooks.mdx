## Abstract

On January 23, 2025, Kalshi, a CFTC-regulated event exchange previously oriented toward political and economic forecasting, officially launched sports markets. Within two months sports contracts accounted for more than half of platform volume. This article examines whether the arrival of a sports-betting user base is associated with measurable changes in trading behavior in the platform's non-sports markets. Using the platform's complete trade tape (approximately 593 million trades collected from mid-2021 through August 2026), I document three facts. First, the share of aggressive (taker) order flow purchasing the low-priced YES side of longshot contracts (1 to 20 cents) rose from 52.1 percent before the launch to 65.7 percent after, a 13.6 percentage-point increase that appears across political, financial, and miscellaneous market categories. Second, the realized excess return to longshot taker positions deteriorated sharply; in the cleanest subsample, markets resolving within 60 days of creation, longshot takers earned +8.3 percent before the launch and lost 21.6 percent after. Third, a counterfactual accounting exercise attributes roughly $13.0 million in additional taker losses to the post-launch shift in loss rates, although this figure is not statistically distinguishable from zero under market-clustered inference and is reported as descriptive. All twelve headline tests are evaluated under a family-wise Holm-Bonferroni correction; eight survive. Null and non-robust results are reported alongside significant ones.

## 1. Introduction

The informational case for prediction markets rests on the composition of their marginal traders. Prices aggregate information efficiently when the marginal trader holds a view about the underlying event; they aggregate demand for lottery-like payoffs when the marginal trader is purchasing skewness. The favorite-longshot bias (FLB), the tendency of bettors to overpay for low-probability outcomes, is among the most extensively documented regularities in wagering markets, and its magnitude in a given market is a useful summary statistic for the composition of that market's demand.

Kalshi provides an unusually clean setting in which to observe a composition change. Before 2025 the platform's users were self-selected consumers of forecasting markets. The official sports launch of January 23, 2025 introduced a product with mass-market appeal, and the platform's account infrastructure imposes no separation between products: a user who registers to bet on football faces zero marginal cost to trade contracts on inflation, elections, or the weather. If sports bettors import sports-betting behavior into event markets, the effect should be visible in exactly three measurable places: the side composition of aggressive order flow, its distribution across the price spectrum, and its subsequent realized returns.

This article measures all three. It is a platform-level event study rather than a randomized design, and I discuss the resulting caveats, including documented pre-trends, in Section 5.

## 2. Data

The analysis uses what is, to my knowledge, the largest public dataset of prediction-market activity, collected with an open-source indexing framework ([Becker, prediction-market-analysis](https://github.com/Jon-Becker/prediction-market-analysis)). The August 2026 refresh performed for this article brings the totals to approximately 593 million Kalshi trades and 30 million market records spanning June 2021 through August 4, 2026. Each trade record identifies the aggressing (taker) side, both contract prices, and the traded quantity; market records supply resolution outcomes and timing.

Three properties of the collection process are material to the design.

**Retention.** Kalshi's public API deletes trade history older than approximately 72 days. I verified this directly: a market with millions of recorded trades returns data for May 26, 2026 and returns nothing for May 5, 2026. As a consequence, the tape is consistently collected through May 1, 2026, has an unrecoverable gap from May 1 to May 24, and is complete from May 25, 2026 onward. Researchers intending to use Kalshi data should collect it continuously.

**Regime comparability.** By mid-2026 Kalshi listed tens of millions of minute-horizon markets (predominantly short-dated cryptocurrency price ladders) and cleared on the order of eight million trades per day, roughly two orders of magnitude above the 2024 rate. Because the post-gap tape differs from the historical tape in both collection methodology and platform regime, no statistical test in this article pools data across the May 2026 boundary. All inferential results use the consistently collected sample ending May 1, 2026, combined with market resolutions as of August 2026. The post-gap era is summarized separately in Section 6.

**Universe definition.** All results restrict to markets with at least 100 contracts of lifetime volume, matching the historical collection rule, so that the estimation universe is stable across the sample. Sports and non-sports classification uses a curated ticker-prefix taxonomy applied longest-prefix-first; keyword-based classification was rejected after auditing (substring matching misclassifies, for example, UKRAINE-prefixed tickers under a precipitation keyword).

## 3. Empirical design

**Treatment date.** The treatment date is January 23, 2025, the official sports launch. The first sports market appeared in October 2024, but volume before the official launch was negligible: sports first exceeded 25 percent of platform contract volume in the week of March 17, 2025 and 50 percent the following week, reaching a maximum weekly share of 92 percent in late 2025.

![Kalshi sports weekly volume, cumulative share, and sports share of total platform volume, 2024 to 2026](/img/blog/kalshi/sports-ramp.png)

**Pre-period composition.** The pre-period includes the 2024 United States election, the highest-volume and most retail-intensive episode in the platform's history prior to the launch. For the returns results this choice is conservative: election-period longshot losses raise the pre-period mean loss rate and therefore shrink the measured post-launch deterioration. Restricting the pre-period to the twelve months preceding the launch yields a larger composition shift (+16.8 percentage points) and a somewhat smaller but still large returns deterioration (−28.8 percentage points, p = 0.003). Both windows are reported in the replication files.

**Outcomes and inference.** The two primary outcomes are (i) the YES-taker share of contracts on markets whose YES contract trades between 1 and 20 cents, and (ii) the dollar-weighted taker excess return on positions entered at 1 to 20 cents, computed as (payout − entry price) / entry price on resolved markets, following Buergi, Deng and Whelan (2025). Weekly series are tested with Newey-West (lag 4) regressions on a post indicator, complemented by block-bootstrap p-values; panel specifications use market fixed effects with cluster-robust standard errors at the market level; dollar-loss aggregates use a market-clustered bootstrap. Twelve headline tests form the inferential family and carry a Holm-Bonferroni family-wise correction. The full test battery, including diagnostics and failed specifications, comprises sixty rows in the replication output.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Composition of aggressive order flow

Among non-sports trades in which the YES contract was priced between 1 and 20 cents, the aggressing side purchased YES 52.1 percent of the time before the launch and 65.7 percent after (+13.6 percentage points; Holm-adjusted p ≈ 10⁻⁶; block-bootstrap p = 0.002). The gradient of YES-taker share with respect to contract price steepened correspondingly (Holm-adjusted p ≈ 2×10⁻⁶): flow tilted toward cheap contracts across the entire price spectrum, not only within the lowest bucket.

![Longshot YES-taker share around the sports launch, and taker excess returns by entry price bucket, pre versus post](/img/blog/kalshi/composition-and-flb.png)

The shift is not attributable to any single market category. Under the curated classification, the post-launch increase in YES-taker share is +8.2 percentage points in political markets, +9.9 in financial and economic markets, and +5.9 in the residual category (each Holm-significant at the 0.002 level).

Two further specifications refine the interpretation:

- **Intensive versus extensive margin.** Restricting to markets that traded on both sides of the launch date, absorbing market fixed effects, controlling for market age, and clustering standard errors by market, the within-market estimate is −1.2 percentage points with p = 0.53. The aggregate composition shift is therefore an extensive-margin phenomenon: it operates through the changing composition of markets and the flow arriving in newly created markets, rather than through deterioration of flow within pre-existing markets. This pattern is consistent with a mechanism operating through new users and new market types rather than repricing of existing instruments.
- **Duration-restricted subsample.** Restricting both periods to markets resolved within 60 days of creation, which removes slow-resolving election-style markets and any settlement-censoring artifacts, the composition shift is +8.0 percentage points (Holm-adjusted p ≈ 10⁻⁴).

### 4.2 Returns to longshot flow

A notable pre-launch fact is the near-absence of a favorite-longshot bias on Kalshi. In the 60-day-resolution subsample, longshot taker positions earned a positive excess return of +8.3 percent before the launch. This stands in contrast to the large negative longshot returns documented across most wagering markets and suggests the platform's early users were unusually price-disciplined at low probabilities.

After the launch, this changed on every measure examined:

| Specification | Pre | Post | Holm-adj. p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly equal-weighted excess return, 1-20c takers | −2.5% | −42.2% | 6×10⁻⁵ |
| Pooled dollar-weighted loss rate, 1-20c | −37.8% | −47.6% | descriptive |
| Duration-restricted (60-day) excess return | +8.3% | −21.6% | 1×10⁻⁶ |
| Post-window restricted to after 2025-09-01 | | | HAC p = 0.003; bootstrap p = 0.028 |

The pooled dollar-weighted figures, which allow high-volume weeks to dominate, are the appropriate welfare-relevant magnitudes; the weekly series is the time-series test. The two weightings differ because election-period volume dominates pooled pre-period dollars. The duration-restricted estimate is the cleanest: within a stable population of fast-resolving markets, the sign of the longshot premium inverted.

An alternative interpretation deserves consideration: cheap YES contracts may have become objectively worse purchases after the launch for reasons unrelated to who was buying them. A supply-side or news-arrival explanation of this kind would imply symmetric changes for passive counterparties. Instead, the maker-taker return gap widened sharply after the launch (joint test p ≈ 3×10⁻⁴), which is the signature of a demand-composition change.

### 4.3 Counterfactual loss accounting

Applying pre-launch per-price-bucket loss rates to post-launch dollar volumes implies expected taker losses of approximately $12.7 million in non-sports markets after January 2025. Realized losses were approximately $25.7 million, an excess of $13.0 million. Decomposed by price bucket, $5.4 million of the excess arises in the 1-20 cent bucket, and $16.2 million arises in the 61-80 cent bucket, where takers earned positive returns before the launch and approximately zero after; retail's pre-launch edge on favorites also disappeared.

![Taker loss per dollar wagered by entry price bucket pre versus post, and cumulative taker dollar losses on non-sports markets](/img/blog/kalshi/retail-losses.png)

These figures are reported as accounting, not as hypothesis tests. An earlier version of this analysis assigned the excess a p-value below 10⁻⁶ using a test that treated each dollar as an independent observation. That procedure is invalid: dollars within a market settle jointly, so the correct clustering unit is the market. Under a market-clustered bootstrap the excess is not statistically distinguishable from zero (p ≈ 0.27), because aggregate dollar losses are dominated by a small number of very large markets. The composition and returns results of Sections 4.1 and 4.2 are the statistically supported findings; the dollar figure is the magnitude they plausibly imply.

## 5. Threats to identification

**Pre-trends.** The longshot YES-taker share was already rising during 2024, and the twelve-month pre-launch linear trend in both primary outcomes is statistically significant. Much of this drift coincides with the election-cycle ramp. Three pieces of evidence bound the concern. A placebo test within the stable 2023 period shows no effect (p = 0.66). The results survive restriction of the post-period to after September 2025, by which time sports constituted roughly 74 percent of platform volume and the election cycle was eleven months past. And the composition shift survives exclusion of the largest post-period markets. A reader who attributes part of the measured magnitude to a post-election user-base persistence effect rather than sports crossover has a coherent partial story; the twelve-month-pre and late-post-window estimates bound its size.

**Cross-platform control.** A difference-in-differences design using Polymarket as a control platform was constructed and rejected: the pre-treatment log-volume series of the two platforms diverge significantly before the treatment date (t = 8.4 on the pre-period trend of the difference), so the parallel-trends assumption fails and no cross-platform estimate is reported.

**Sample-edge selection.** Return calculations require resolution. Because unresolved markets are censored, the duration-restricted specification of Section 4.2, which conditions on markets resolving within 60 days of creation in both periods, is the primary defense against resolution-selection artifacts; the headline result survives it.

**Fees.** Trading fees are not modeled. Their inclusion would make all taker return figures more negative.

## 6. The 2026 platform

The post-gap tape (May 25 to August 4, 2026) describes a platform transformed a second time. Kalshi now clears roughly eight million trades per day; esports alone accounts for approximately 23 percent of contract volume, and minute- and fifteen-minute cryptocurrency ladders dominate the non-sports segment. In this regime the longshot YES-taker share in non-sports markets is 72.9 percent, higher than any value in the estimation sample, and the pooled longshot taker excess return is −16.9 percent. Because collection methodology differs across the boundary, I do not test these against the earlier sample; descriptively, nothing in the new era suggests reversion of the patterns documented above.

## 7. Conclusion

Between 2021 and early 2025, Kalshi's non-sports markets exhibited an unusually mild favorite-longshot bias, consistent with a self-selected population of forecasting-oriented traders. Following the January 2025 sports launch, aggressive retail flow in those same market categories tilted strongly and persistently toward low-probability contracts, and the realized return to that flow deteriorated to levels comparable with conventional betting markets. The change operated through the extensive margin, consistent with the arrival of new users and new market types rather than behavioral change within existing markets. The efficiency function of the platform's prices need not be impaired by these findings, since professional liquidity provision continues to discipline quotes; the welfare experience of the marginal retail participant, however, measurably worsened at precisely the moment the platform achieved mass-market scale.

All code, data collection infrastructure, and the complete sixty-test result set, including specifications that failed, are available in the [public repository](https://github.com/Jon-Becker/prediction-market-analysis).
